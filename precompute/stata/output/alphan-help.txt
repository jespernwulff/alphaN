      Title
      
          alphan -- Set the significance level as a function of the sample size,
                      using Bayes factors
      
      
      Syntax
      
          Calibrate the significance level
      
              alphan numlist [, calibration_options]
      
          Power at the calibrated level
      
              alphan power numlist, d(numlist) [calibration_options]
      
          Settings report for a preregistration
      
              alphan report # [, calibration_options saving(filename [, replace])
                      width(#) powerat(numlist)]
      
          Convert test results into Bayes factors
      
              alphan jab [coefficients] [, method(name) upper(#) n(#)]
      
              alphan jabt numlist, n(numlist) [method(name) upper(#)]
      
              alphan jabp numlist, n(numlist) [df(numlist) method(name) upper(#)]
      
              alphan klauerbf, n(numlist) {t(numlist) | fstat(numlist)} [q(#) p(#)
                      method(name) de(#) nu(#) r(#)]
      
          Graphs
      
              alphan plot [, bf(#) max(#) ylim(# #) methods(namelist) de(#)
                      log(x|y|xy) twoway_options]
      
              alphan powerplot [, d(numlist) bf(#) max(#) methods(namelist) de(#)
                      ref(#) noref twoway_options]
      
              alphan jabplot # [, bf(#) method(name) upper(#) twoway_options]
      
          Effective sample size for clustered data
      
              alphan n_effective, n(numlist) se(numlist) se_robust(numlist)
      
          Version of the command
      
              alphan version
      
      
          calibration_options     Description
          --------------------------------------------------------------------------
            bf(numlist)           target Bayes factor; default is bf(1) (bf(3) for
                                    alphan power)
            method(name)          jab (the default), min, robust, balanced, es, or
                                    moment
            upper(#)              upper limit of the realistic effect sizes; method
                                    balanced only; default is upper(1)
            de(#)                 targeted effect size; methods es and moment;
                                    default is de(.5)
            nu(#)                 degrees of freedom of the prior; methods es and
                                    moment; default is 3 for es, q+4 for moment
            r(#)                  scale of the prior; method es; default is
                                    sqrt((nu-2)/(nu q))*de
            q(#)                  number of coefficients tested jointly; methods es
                                    and moment; default is q(1)
            p(#)                  number of parameters retained under the null,
                                    including any intercept; methods es and moment;
                                    default is p(0)
          --------------------------------------------------------------------------
          upper(), de(), nu(), r(), q(), and p() accept numeric expressions, for
            example de(sqrt(.15)). Numeric lists are recycled to the length of the
            longest one.
      
      
      Description
      
          With a fixed significance level such as .05, a large enough sample turns
          almost any p-value below .05 into evidence for the null hypothesis
          (Lindley's paradox). alphan sets the significance level as a decreasing
          function of the sample size, so that a significant result corresponds to a
          Bayes factor of at least bf() in favor of the alternative hypothesis
          (Wulff and Taylor 2024). It is a port of the R package alphaN: it
          implements the same calculations and agrees with the R package to the
          tolerances documented in the package's test suite.
      
          alphan numlist returns the calibrated significance level for each sample
          size. alphan power adds the power of the test at that level against a
          standardized effect. alphan report writes the settings behind a calibrated
          level as a short text to attach to a preregistration.
      
          alphan jab, used after an estimation command, returns Jeffreys'
          approximate Bayes factor for the coefficients of the model (all except the
          intercept, unless you name others). It works after any command that stores
          e(b), e(V), and e(N). A Bayes factor too large for a double (R returns
          Inf) is reported as about 8e+307. alphan jabt and alphan jabp do the same
          from a reported t statistic or p-value. alphan klauerbf returns the
          effect-size or moment Bayes factor of Klauer, Meyer-Grant, and Kellen
          (2025) from a t or F statistic.
      
          alphan plot draws the level against the sample size, alphan powerplot
          draws power against the sample size with every method at its own
          calibrated level, and alphan jabplot draws the Bayes factor against the
          p-value at a given sample size. alphan n_effective computes the effective
          sample size recommended by Wulff and Taylor (2024) for clustered data.
      
      
      Calibration options
      
          bf(numlist) is the Bayes factor to match: 1 to avoid Lindley's paradox, 3
              for moderate evidence, 10 for strong evidence.
      
          method(name) selects the Bayes factor to calibrate to. The first four
              methods invert Jeffreys' approximate Bayes factor and differ in b, the
              fraction of the data used to specify the prior. The last two invert
              the exact test-statistic Bayes factors of Klauer et al. (2025), whose
              priors center the alternative on an effect size you choose.
      
              jab          b = 1/n: Jeffreys' approximate Bayes factor (Wagenmakers
                             2022)
              min          b = 2/n: the minimal training sample (Gu et al. 2018)
              robust       b = max(2/n, 1/sqrt(n)): prevents too small a b (O'Hagan
                             1995)
              balanced     b balances the Type I and Type II errors (Gu et al. 2016)
              es           the effect-size Bayes factor (Klauer et al. 2025)
              moment       the moment Bayes factor (Klauer et al. 2025), which
                             assigns zero density to the null value itself and
                             little to effects near it
      
              Method names are not case sensitive, and any unique abbreviation will
              do.
      
          upper(#) is the upper limit of the range of realistic effect sizes for
              method balanced, which treats them as uniform on [0, upper].
      
          de(#) is the targeted effect size: Cohen's d for q(1) and Cohen's f for
              joint tests. For joint tests, Cohen (1988, chap. 9) labels f^2 of .02,
              .15, and .35 as small, medium, and large, so de(sqrt(.15)) targets a
              medium effect.
      
          nu(#) and r(#) set the degrees of freedom and scale of the prior. The
              defaults are the recommendations of Klauer et al. (2025). The special
              case method(es) nu(1) de(0) r(1) gives the default
              (Jeffreys-Zellner-Siow type) Bayes factor of Rouder et al. (2009).
      
          q(#) is the number of coefficients tested jointly. For q(1) the level
              applies to the two-sided t test of one coefficient; for larger values
              it applies to the F test of the joint null.
      
          p(#) is the number of parameters retained in the model under the null,
              including any intercept. The effective sample size of Klauer et al.
              (2025) is n - p. The default, 0, is the conservative large-sample
              form. For the test of one regression coefficient, set p() to the
              number of other estimated coefficients, including the intercept, to
              obtain the calibration that is exact for the normal linear model.
      
      
      Other options
      
          d(numlist) (alphan power, alphan powerplot) is the standardized effect at
              which power is evaluated, on the same scale as de(): what the
              coefficient's t statistic divided by the square root of the sample
              size estimates. alphan powerplot draws one panel per value; the
              default is d(.1 .5).
      
          n() (alphan jab) replaces e(N) as the sample size, for example with an
              effective sample size. n(numlist) (other subcommands) is the sample
              size.
      
          df(numlist) (alphan jabp) says that the p-value comes from a t test with
              these degrees of freedom. Without it, the p-value is treated as coming
              from a z test.
      
          t(numlist) and fstat(numlist) (alphan klauerbf) are the test statistic.
              With q(1) give exactly one of them (fstat() is read as the squared t
              statistic); with q() above 1 give fstat().
      
          saving(filename [, replace]) (alphan report) also writes the report to a
              file. width(#) sets the maximum line width (default 72; at least 40).
              powerat(numlist) adds the power against each of these standardized
              effects.
      
          max(#) (graphs) is the largest sample size drawn; default 10,000.  ylim(#
              #) sets the range of the vertical axis. methods(namelist) lists the
              methods to draw; the default is jab min robust balanced.  log()
              requests logarithmic axes. ref(#) is the fixed level drawn as a dashed
              reference curve (default .05); noref omits it. Other options are
              passed to twoway, so name(), saving(), and title() work as usual. The
              data in memory are left alone.
      
      
      Choosing a calibration
      
          jab is a good choice when you want to be conservative against small
          effects; min when the maximum likelihood estimate may be misspecified;
          robust when it may be misspecified and the sample is small; and balanced
          when Type II errors are costly. es and moment answer a sharper question:
          which level makes a significant result evidence against an alternative
          centered on the effect size you care about?
      
          Klauer et al. (2025) derive the es and moment Bayes factors under the
          normal linear model (t tests, linear regression, ANOVA). For other
          generalized linear models they hold in the same asymptotic sense as the
          prior-fraction methods, whose derivation covers any such model directly;
          for non-Gaussian models with a modest sample, prefer the prior-fraction
          methods.
      
          With clustered data, Wulff and Taylor (2024) recommend calibrating with
          the total number of observations, which is conservative, and then checking
          whether conclusions survive when alpha and the Bayes factor are recomputed
          with the effective sample size from alphan n_effective.
      
      
      Stored results
      
          Every subcommand except alphan version stores r(table), a matrix with one
          row per case, and r(method) where a method applies. In addition:
      
          alphan numlist   
            r(alpha)              calibrated level (last row)
            r(n), r(BF)           sample size and target (last row)
            r(de), r(nu), r(r), r(q), r(p)
                                    prior settings, methods es and moment
            r(upper)              upper limit, method balanced
      
          alphan power     
            r(power), r(alpha)    power and level (last row)
      
          alphan report    
            r(alpha)              calibrated level
      
          alphan jab       
            r(n)                  sample size used
            r(BF)                 Bayes factor, when one coefficient is reported
            r(table)              columns b, se, t or z, p, BF; rows named after the
                                    coefficients
      
          alphan jabt, alphan jabp, alphan klauerbf
            r(BF)                 Bayes factor in favor of the alternative (last
                                    row)
      
          alphan n_effective
            r(n_effective)        effective sample size (last row)
      
          alphan plot, alphan powerplot
            r(curve)              the calibrated levels drawn
            r(methods)            the methods drawn
      
      
      Examples
      
          Calibrate the level for a regression on 1,000 observations
              . alphan 1000
              . alphan 1000, bf(3)
              . alphan 1000, bf(3) method(balanced)
      
          Calibrate to a Bayes factor that targets a medium effect
              . alphan 100 1000 10000, bf(3) method(es) de(.5)
              . alphan 1000, bf(3) method(moment) de(.5)
      
          A joint F test of two coefficients, with two parameters retained
              . alphan 200, bf(3) method(es) q(2) p(2) de(sqrt(.15))
      
          Bayes factors after a regression
              . sysuse auto
              . regress price mpg weight i.foreign
              . alphan jab
              . alphan jab weight, method(min)
      
          Bayes factors from reported results
              . alphan jabt 2.695, n(200)
              . alphan jabp .007038863, n(200) df(190)
              . alphan klauerbf, n(80) t(2.24) de(.5)
              . alphan klauerbf, n(175) fstat(1.17) q(2) p(3) de(sqrt(.15))
      
          Power, and a report for a preregistration
              . alphan power 1000, d(.1) bf(3)
              . alphan report 1000, bf(3) method(es) p(4) powerat(.1 .5)
      
          Graphs
              . alphan plot, bf(3)
              . alphan plot, bf(3) methods(jab es moment) log(xy)
              . alphan powerplot, d(.1 .5) bf(3) methods(jab balanced moment)
                  max(2000)
              . alphan jabplot 1000, bf(3)
      
          Clustered data: classical and cluster-robust standard errors of one
          coefficient
              . webuse nlswork
              . regress ln_wage ttl_exp tenure south
              . local se = _se[south]
              . regress ln_wage ttl_exp tenure south, vce(cluster idcode)
              . alphan n_effective, n(`=e(N)') se(`se') se_robust(`=_se[south]')
              . alphan jab south, n(`=r(n_effective)')
